How this estimate works

Compared with supplying the appliances directly from mains. Appliance consumption is estimated from the plug meter, battery current, inverter idle draw and configured efficiencies. Costs include spot prices, VAT and your extra variable charges.

All figures cover the same recorded periods. Estimated direct mains is what supplying the appliances directly from the grid would have cost at each period’s electricity price. Metered grid is the cost of electricity actually measured through the plug, including charging, at the price when it was drawn.

The battery-energy adjustment is the value of the net change in stored energy, not the total value of the energy currently in the battery. A negative adjustment means more battery energy was used than put back during the recorded periods. It is subtracted so energy already stored before the period does not appear free. A positive adjustment credits net energy added to the battery, which is still available for later use.

For example, an adjustment of −0.84 kr at a reference value of 1.72 kr/kWh means about 0.49 kWh more was taken out of the battery than put back. It does not mean the energy left in the battery is worth only 0.84 kr.

The fixed stored-energy value is an accounting reference, not the actual price paid for each charge. It is set when tracking begins using a reference electricity price adjusted for charging losses, then kept constant. Actual charging costs are already included in metered grid at the prices paid. When equal amounts of stored energy are added and used within the recorded periods, the adjustment cancels to zero. Charging can temporarily lower the savings figure; using that energy later can raise it. These are estimated operating savings, not a measured bill reduction; equipment cost and battery wear are excluded.

Green bars mean savings; red bars mean extra cost. Missing observations and unknown prices are excluded, not counted as zero savings. Today and days with gaps are partial. Phone Bluetooth access

Temporarily disconnect the plug from the Daly BMS so your phone can connect, without unplugging mains. Mains stays on for up to five minutes, including charging. Live BMS readings and the plug’s battery fault checks are unavailable during this window; the BMS’s own protection remains active. Meter and overcurrent stops still apply.

Waiting for connection status.

Start with mains enabled and healthy BMS readings; select Always charging first if necessary. Close the Daly app and disconnect your phone’s Bluetooth connection before reconnecting here. Automatic reconnection starts after five minutes, with up to 45 seconds to receive a new reading. If reconnection fails, normal stale-BMS shutdown applies. This window does not survive a plug restart.

What is recorded?

The latest 32 snapshots are saved locally when charge permission changes, or its switch/alarm details change while charging is prohibited. A baseline is also saved after startup. Each snapshot is committed to flash immediately and survives power cycles; the oldest is overwritten. Very brief events between the 10-second BMS readings can be missed.

Charge permission is a software safety check, not a “battery full” flag. Raw switch values and alarm bits are retained without filtering. Battery values and plug state describe the reading before the controller responds. These records do not change charging behaviour.
